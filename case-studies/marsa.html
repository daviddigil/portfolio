MARSA

A coastal coffee and croissants brand built for Alexandria, Egypt — a full identity guideline, a 3D mascot character, and applications across environmental, delivery, stationery and a photoreal social content series shot against the city's own skyline.

RoleGraphic & Brand Designer
ScopeFull identity system
PlatformPrint, environmental, social
ClientMARSA Coffee & Croissants, Alexandria

The brief

MARSA needed a brand that felt specifically coastal and Alexandrian — not a generic "coffee shop blue" identity that could belong to any city. At the same time it needed the full practical kit a real café runs on: a complete brand guideline a third party could follow without me in the room, a mascot character flexible enough to carry personality across social content, and applications proven out across everything from a receipt book to a delivery rider's jacket.

Approach

I built the identity around a bold royal blue and sun-yellow pairing — a combination that reads as bright, coastal and appetite-friendly rather than corporate, and holds up equally well on a dark storefront sign and a yellow delivery bag. The logo itself resolves two ideas into one mark: a heart shape and a pair of croissants, so "coffee" and "croissants" are legible in the icon before anyone reads the wordmark.

Because this was built as a formal guideline rather than a one-off logo file, I documented the system the way a brand actually needs to be handed off: correct logo usage alongside common logo mistakes, a geometric construction grid, a defined safe zone, color variations for black/white contexts, a custom brand pattern, and typography — so the system is reproducible by someone who isn't me.

What I built

A full identity system: brand guideline, environmental and delivery applications, stationery, and a photoreal social content series — not a logo with a handful of mockups.

Operational collateral

The system also had to work as the actual paperwork and signage a café runs on day to day — not just the glamorous applications.

Social content

The identity extends into a photoreal social series shot against Alexandria's actual landmarks — Stanley Bridge, the Corniche, Qaitbay-style coastal architecture — tagged with real neighbourhood locations (El Maamoura), so the brand reads as genuinely local rather than a stock-photo café.

Outcome

A complete, documented brand system — a guideline a third party could actually follow, not just a logo file — proven out across environmental, delivery, stationery and a location-grounded social content series.
