Aqaar

Aqaar helps first-time home seekers find, compare and book a viewing, without the noise of buying, financing or investment tools getting in the way. A property-search experience built for Cairo renters, not investors.

RoleUI/UX Designer
Timeline2026
PlatformMobile & Web App
MarketEgypt (Cairo)

The challenge

Users can feel overwhelmed searching through hundreds of property listings, making it difficult to find relevant options, remember favorites, and compare properties based on their needs. Existing Egyptian real-estate apps are built around features that matter to investors and agents, not the person just trying to find one apartment to actually live in.

Market & competitor research

A SWOT analysis and a feature-by-feature competitor comparison (against Nawy, Aqarmap and Property Finder) showed where Aqaar could actually differentiate: clean, focused filters; an easy-to-learn, side-by-side comparison view; and self-serve visit scheduling: features that existing apps either lacked or buried. A structured user survey of 15 questions and 96 answers from people who'd searched for an apartment in Cairo in the past 6 months backed this up, with 81% of respondents aged 24–38 and first-time home seekers.

Interviews, persona & empathy map

Twelve one-on-one interviews with people who'd recently searched for an apartment in Cairo surfaced two consistent themes (an affinity map grouped around Time & Effort and Trust & Uncertainty), which converged on a single primary persona: Ahmed Hassan, 31, a software engineer working in New Cairo, whose goals, frustrations, needs and behaviors shaped the rest of the design decisions.

User journey & flows

Ahmed's end-to-end journey (from realizing he needs to move, to walking into a viewing he actually chose) was mapped emotionally (motivated → curious → hopeful → overwhelmed → confident → relieved → satisfied) and storyboarded as four real scenarios, then turned into concrete step-by-step flows for the two actions that matter most: searching and saving a property, and booking a visit.

Design system

Mid-fidelity sketches and high-fidelity wireframes led into a documented design system, built to keep visual consistency and speed up development by standardizing colors, typography, grids, components and interaction patterns across the whole product. Outfit is the primary typeface, on a five-tone palette anchored by a lime-green brand color (#D8E171) against warm cream and near-black neutrals.

UI design

The UI phase transformed the UX structure into clear, functional screens with a simple visual hierarchy and consistent components: Home (search, location chips, recommended properties), a Nav Bar organized into five core sections, Profile, Map View (price-tagged pins), Property Details, Create Account, and Filters, plus a full dark mode variant of the entire screen set.

Website & agent partner dashboard

Beyond the consumer app, Aqaar extends to a public marketing website: a warm cream-and-lime landing page with featured categories, a "three steps, zero chasing" explainer, and a guides/FAQ section, plus a separate agent-side companion product, Aqaar Partner, deliberately kept simple: an overview dashboard, a leads/inquiries list, a visit calendar, and a guided property-listing form with a live preview.

Outcome

A full property-search ecosystem designed around one deliberately narrow promise: find, compare and visit, without the noise of buying, financing or investment tools. It was validated through 12 interviews and a 96-response survey, then carried consistently from a single persona through to consumer app, dark mode, marketing website and agent dashboard.

The discipline here was saying no to feature creep. Aqaar stays a first-time home seeker's tool, not an investor's, and every design decision (filters, comparison, scheduling) traces back to a pain point the research actually surfaced.
