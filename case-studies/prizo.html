Concept Grocery Price Comparison · UI/UX

Prizo

A grocery price-comparison app that helps shoppers find the lowest price for their whole basket across multiple platforms, instead of checking each app one product at a time. Built from in-depth user research through to a full UI system.

RoleUI/UX Designer
Timeline2026
PlatformMobile app
IndustryGrocery

The challenge

Grocery shoppers already know prices vary across platforms. The problem is that checking them doesn't scale. Comparing prices one product at a time is slow, most apps only show the current price for a single item rather than an entire basket, and there's no simple way to tell whether a "deal" has actually improved or just moved. The result: users either overpay out of fatigue, or spend more time comparison-shopping than the savings are worth.

Research process

The project followed a structured path from empathy through to testing, mapped out as a full design-process timeline from February through August, covering Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Testing phases rather than jumping straight into UI.

Personas & empathy mapping

Research surfaced two distinct user profiles rather than one composite persona: Shravan, a content creator comparing prices before every purchase, and Bhargavi, a working professional in Bangalore managing a household grocery budget across multiple apps. Both share the same underlying goal (compare the total cart price across platforms in one place) but arrive at it from different daily routines, which shaped two separate empathy maps.

Flows & wireframes

With the problem and users defined, the structure came next: an information architecture covering five core sections (Home, Categories, Compare, Wishlist, Profile), a user flow from first open through to a finished comparison cart, and mid-fidelity wireframes for the key screens before any visual design was applied.

Visual system

The UI runs on SF Pro for clarity across dense product listings and price comparisons, on a tight five-colour palette anchored by Dark Leaf Green (#318616), with Mint Mist, Sage Gray, Sage Green and white as supporting tones. A 3-column, 16px-gutter grid keeps every screen consistent, and a small, consistent icon set keeps the comparison-heavy interface legible rather than cluttered.

UI design journey

Each screen was designed for clarity, consistency and ease of use, carrying the research directly into interface decisions. The home screen brings categories, deals and quick search together with an AI assistant to keep price comparison simple; the comparison and smart-cart screens are where the actual saving happens.

Outcome

A fully researched and designed comparison-shopping flow, from a quantified, interview-backed problem statement through two personas, mapped flows, a documented design system and a complete screen set, addressing the three pain points the research actually found: cart-level optimization, price-drop visibility, and full-basket comparison in one place.

The real design decision here wasn't any single screen. It was choosing to optimize the whole cart across stores, instead of one product at a time, because that's the exact gap the research showed every existing app was missing.
