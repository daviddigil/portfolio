CycleMate

A mobile app for renting bikes and tracking rides — redesigned from a cluttered, inconsistent UI into a clean, research-backed system, with the full 340-hour design process documented end to end.

RoleUI/UX Designer
Timeline340 hours · April 2025
PlatformMobile app (iOS)
LocationCalifornia, USA

The challenge

CycleMate's existing app had a specific, diagnosable problem rather than a vague one: a cluttered UI and inconsistent iconography made it hard for users to find key features, which showed up directly as a poor, confusing experience at exactly the moments that mattered most — finding a bike, checking its stats, and booking it.

Research & process

Rather than jumping straight to visuals, the process was structured and time-boxed across three phases, documented in hours rather than vague sprints: Discovery (60h) covering user research, competitor analysis and ideation; Design Process (120h) covering wireframing, user flow mapping and prototyping; and Solution (160h) covering branding, UX, UI, design-system checks, high-fidelity wireframing and usability testing — 340 hours of documented work in total.

Wireframes to UI

Low-fidelity wireframes came first, covering the full screen set end to end, before any visual design decisions were layered on top — the structural problem (navigation, hierarchy, findability) had to be solved independently of colour and type.

Key flows

Three flows carry most of the product's actual value, so they got the most design attention: getting a new user signed up and into the app with minimal friction, finding and booking a specific bike with real specs, and a safety feature that's easy to miss in a bike-rental product but matters a lot — knowing where your bike is if something goes wrong.

Visual system & branding

The visual language is built around Manrope as the primary typeface and a high-contrast lime-on-near-black palette (#BEEE02 on #202411, with #D0E08B and white as supporting tones) — energetic and legible outdoors, which matters for an app people check mid-ride in daylight. The logo itself is a simple cyclist-in-motion mark, applied consistently from the app icon down to physical branded merchandise.

Outcome

A complete, research-backed redesign — not a visual refresh layered on the old structure, but a rebuilt information architecture with the UI following from it. These are the project's own reported results, presented as Behance case-study metrics rather than independently audited figures:

340hdocumented design process, Discovery to Solution
10K+rides tracked (reported)
95%customer satisfaction (reported)

The real discipline on display here is process, not just the final screens — a cluttered-UI problem was diagnosed specifically, then solved through a structured, time-tracked sequence from research through wireframes to a finished, branded product.
